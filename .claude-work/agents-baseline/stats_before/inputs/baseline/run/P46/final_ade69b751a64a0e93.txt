- **Ridge:** the bad fold comes from extreme ratio rows (AveOccup goes up to 1243) that the linear model extrapolates on. Taking log1p of those columns fixes it, so I added that as a fifth model.
- **Gradient boosting vs log-Ridge:** both boosters are better by 0.20 RMSE (CI [-0.217, -0.187]; 5x2cv t and F tests p < 1e-4).
- **LightGBM vs HGB:** LightGBM is 0.0035 lower and wins 10 of 10 folds, but the corrected CI is [-0.009, 0.002] (p = 0.17). Dietterich's 5x2 t-test gives p = 0.026 and the F-test p = 0.078. In practice there is no difference (0.7% of RMSE).
- **Spatial check:** random folds overstate accuracy because nearby districts look alike. When 0.5° map cells (176 of them) are kept together in one fold, both boosters lose about 0.16 RMSE. They then beat log-Ridge by only 0.05–0.07, which is not detectable (CI spans 0).
- **Chosen model:** HGB, because it matches LightGBM statistically and needs no extra dependency. I saved it in `model/`, and a reload-and-predict smoke test passed.

**Calibration note** (held-out halves, mean over 10 folds):
- **Point predictions:** the boosters are well calibrated. Regressing y on the prediction gives slope 1.006 to 1.008 and intercept -0.013 to -0.016, and the largest gap between mean y and mean prediction across prediction deciles is 0.037. Log-Ridge has a slope of 0.998 but a 0.29 decile gap, so its errors are systematic: the linear form misses curvature in the features.
- **90% intervals:** I built split-conformal intervals (|residual| quantile from 5-fold CV inside the training half; half-width about 0.72 for the boosters). Overall coverage is 0.90 to 0.903, but only about 0.79 to 0.80 in the top predicted decile and about 0.69 on the 992 rows capped at 5.0. That is because the error spread grows with the predicted price and the target is cut off at 5.0. If intervals will be used per house, switch to normalised conformal or conformalised quantile regression, and treat the 5.0 cap as censored.
