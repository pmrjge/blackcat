**Theorem.** Suppose $(P,p_1,p_2)$ and $(Q,q_1,q_2)$ are both products of $A$ and $B$. Then exactly one morphism $\varphi\colon P\to Q$ satisfies $q_i\circ\varphi=p_i$ for $i=1,2$, and that $\varphi$ is an isomorphism. So it is the unique isomorphism that respects the projections.

**Lemma.** If $(P,p_1,p_2)$ is a product and $e\colon P\to P$ satisfies $p_i\circ e=p_i$, then $e=\mathrm{id}_P$.
*Proof:* Apply the universal property to the pair $(p_1,p_2)$. Both $e$ and $\mathrm{id}_P$ satisfy it, and the universal property allows only one such map, so they are equal.

**Proof of the theorem.**
1. The universal property of $Q$, applied to $(p_1,p_2)$, gives a unique $\varphi$ with $q_i\varphi=p_i$.
2. The universal property of $P$, applied to $(q_1,q_2)$, gives a unique $\psi$ with $p_i\psi=q_i$.
3. $p_i(\psi\varphi)=q_i\varphi=p_i$, so $\psi\varphi=\mathrm{id}_P$ by the Lemma. In the same way, $q_i(\varphi\psi)=q_i$ gives $\varphi\psi=\mathrm{id}_Q$.
4. Any isomorphism $\theta\colon P\to Q$ with $q_i\theta=p_i$ satisfies the condition that defines $\varphi$, so $\theta=\varphi$. In fact this holds for any such morphism, not just isomorphisms.

The PDF also contains:
- **What "unique" means:** uniqueness only holds relative to the projections. In **Set**, the swap map on $A\times A$ is an automorphism other than the identity, but it does not commute with $\pi_1,\pi_2$.
- **The converse:** if $(P,p_1,p_2)$ is a product and $\theta\colon Q\to P$ is an isomorphism, then $(Q,p_1\theta,p_2\theta)$ is also a product.
- **Two other routes to the same result:**
  - A product is a terminal object in the category of cones over $(A,B)$, and terminal objects are unique up to unique isomorphism.
  - By Yoneda: $\mathcal C(-,P)\cong\mathcal C(-,A)\times\mathcal C(-,B)$, and two such representations are related by a unique isomorphism.
