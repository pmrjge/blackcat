VERDICT: fail

The plan can't meet a 5-minute downtime budget for an 800 GB table. As written, it also loses data and deletes the only fallback.

FINDINGS (most severe first):

- **[BLOCKING] Step 1 before step 2 loses data.** The dump is taken while the app is still writing. Every write between the dump's snapshot and "stop app" is not on the new server, and with this table that window lasts hours. If you swap the order, those hours become downtime instead.

- **[BLOCKING] The 5-minute budget is impossible with dump and restore.** Parallel `pg_dump -j` dumps several *tables* at once. It does not split one table, so an 800 GB table goes through one worker (PG16 docs, app-pgdump: "dumping njobs tables simultaneously"). `pg_restore -j` has the same limit for that table's data. Even at a sustained 500 MB/s, the data copy alone takes about 27 minutes. Rebuilding the indexes, validating constraints and running `ANALYZE` adds more, typically hours. Fix: keep the bulk copy out of the downtime window.
  - Use logical replication (publication and subscription) from the old server to PG16. Copy the schema with `pg_dump --schema-only`, let the initial sync run while the app stays up, and cut over once lag is near 0.
  - This needs `wal_level=logical` on the source, which takes a restart if it isn't already set. Schedule that restart separately.
  - The table needs a primary key or a `REPLICA IDENTITY`.
  - If the source is a managed service, check whether it has a built-in blue/green option.

- **[BLOCKING] Step 5 is destructive, irreversible and has no gate.** Deleting the old server removes the only rollback target. It needs explicit user consent and must come after a defined soak period on the new server, plus a verified backup of the new one (a backup counts only after a test restore). Until then, keep the old server stopped or read-only, not deleted.

- **[BLOCKING] There is no rollback plan.** Nothing defines when to abort or how to go back: revert the connection target, and decide whether writes made on PG16 after cutover are lost or sent back by reverse replication.

- **[BLOCKING] There is no verification and no done-when for any step.** It needs objective checks:
  - row counts per table, and checksums or sampled hashes of the big table;
  - replication lag of 0 at cutover (`pg_stat_subscription`, or comparing `pg_current_wal_lsn()` with the subscriber's position);
  - app smoke tests and error rates after the switch;
  - a timed rehearsal of the whole cutover on a copy of production.

- **[BLOCKING] A DNS change as the cutover risks split-brain and can blow the 5 minutes on its own.** Clients and connection pools cache the old address beyond the TTL, and long-lived connections stay open. Fix:
  - Lower the TTL days in advance, or better, switch a PgBouncer, proxy or VIP target or the app config.
  - Fence the old server at cutover (`default_transaction_read_only`, `pg_hba` reject, or terminating backends) so late writers fail loudly instead of writing to the old copy.

- **[BLOCKING] Load-bearing facts are not stated or checked.**
  - The source version is unknown. Upgrade, same version and downgrade behave differently; going from a newer major version to PG16 isn't a supported path for logical dumps.
  - It's unknown whether the extensions are available on PG16.
  - It's unknown whether large objects are used. Logical replication does not copy them (PG16 docs, logical-replication-restrictions).
  - Disk space for the dump plus the restore has not been checked.

- **[non-blocking] Sequences are not copied by logical replication** (same docs page). The cutover must `setval` every sequence to at least the source value, or new inserts hit duplicate keys.

- **[non-blocking] An hours-long dump or initial sync holds back the xmin horizon on production,** which causes bloat and extra load. Watch it, and run it off-peak or from a replica if the method allows.

- **[non-blocking] Why PG16?** 18 is the current major version. If the move is an upgrade anyway, targeting 18 avoids a second migration sooner.

- **[non-blocking] Owners, cost and secrets are missing.** No step names who does it. The cost of running both servers during the soak period is not mentioned. Connection credentials for the new server should come from a secret store, not from scripts.
