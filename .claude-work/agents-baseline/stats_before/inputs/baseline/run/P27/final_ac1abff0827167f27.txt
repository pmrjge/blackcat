Model and conventions:
- 5 qubits: data qubits 0, 2, 4 and ancillas 1, 3. The CNOTs are noiseless and the logical qubit starts in |0>.
- Each round puts a bit flip (stim `X_ERROR(p)`) on the data qubits before syndrome extraction and on the ancillas before their measure-and-reset (`MR`). The final data readout also gets `X_ERROR(p)`.
- 8 detectors: the first round compares ancillas with nothing, later rounds compare with the previous round, and the final round checks the data parities against the last syndrome.
- The logical observable is Z on data qubit 4.
- The "data only" row is the same circuit without the measurement flips.
- Both decoders run on the same sampled shots, so they are a paired comparison.

EVIDENCE:
- **Circuit checks:** the circuit with noise removed gives zero detection events in 1,000 shots, and building the error model with `decompose_errors=True` works.
- **Exact enumeration:** the error model has 18 equal-probability mechanisms (2^18 configurations; 9 in the data-only case). Decoding every configuration exactly gives the "exact value" column. The simulated rates agree with it at z = +0.36 (MWPM) and +0.22 (majority); data-only z = +0.92 and +1.30.
- **Majority vote, analytic:** each data qubit's net flip probability is q = (1 − (1−2p)^4)/2 with measurement errors (exponent 3 without). The logical error is then 3q² − 2q³ = 0.00440, or 0.00254 for data only, matching the enumeration.
- **Decoder check, independent of PyMatching:**
  - A brute-force minimum-weight decoder gives 0.002893 and the maximum-likelihood decoder 0.002871.
  - `tiecheck.py` shows PyMatching never predicts outside the minimum-weight class. Its higher rate comes from 136 of the 256 syndromes, where equally light explanations disagree on the logical value.
  - With data-only noise there are no such ties, and MWPM, brute force and maximum likelihood all give 0.000893.
