RESULT: I've drawn the fox, written the plan and prepared the stencil files. It's a front-facing geometric fox head with 10 facets, symmetric, at 56 × 63 mm (line centres; about 57 × 64 mm of ink). Every line is 1.2 mm black (#000000, RGB 0/0/0); the ear tips, nose and eyes are solid black. It goes on the upper, wider half of the forearm with the ears toward the elbow, so it reads upright to others when the arm hangs and to the wearer palm-up.
- **Size vs line weight over 10 years:** I assumed each line spreads 0.15–0.25 mm per side over 10 years. To stay legible, a gap must still be at least as wide as the line next to it in year 10. That means at least 2.2 mm of clear skin between lines at year 0.
  - The delivered 63 mm fox has 3.28 mm at its tightest gap (eye to cheek line).
  - 50 mm is the smallest that works (2.36 mm).
  - At 40 mm the eyes merge into the cheek line, so it would need 0.8 mm lines and fewer facets.
  - When rescaling, keep the line at 1.2 mm and scale only the shapes.
- **Lettering rule:** use the same 2.2 mm gap rule. With a 1.2 mm single-weight sans, that means:
  - lowercase at least 6 mm tall (capitals only: at least 7 mm), with at least 2.4 mm between letters;
  - script at least 8 mm tall;
  - text running along the forearm, at least 8 mm from the fox;
  - wording confirmed in writing, and a licensed font (desktop licence or SIL OFL) with its terms checked.
- **Why this design:** the bold silhouette and solid black at the tips age better than fine detail. The facets were sized from the gap rule rather than chosen by eye. I moved the eyes once, after the first version left only 1.88 mm to the cheek line. Narrow corners are either solid on purpose or measured.
