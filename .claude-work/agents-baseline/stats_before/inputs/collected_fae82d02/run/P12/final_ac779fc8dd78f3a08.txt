I wrote the schema, the queries and a test suite for the double-entry ledger. All 10 tests pass under SQLite 3.54.0.

The failing-insert proof is `test_unbalanced_post_fails`. It adds a debit of 1000 and a credit of 999, then tries to post. The post is rejected with "transaction does not balance (debits <> credits, or fewer than 2 entries)". The transaction stays a draft and `posted_entries` stays empty.

**How the balance rule works.** SQLite has no deferred constraints. So a transaction starts as a draft, and entries are added to it one at a time. Changing its status to `posted` runs the `transactions_balance` trigger. That trigger aborts unless there are at least 2 entries and the debit sum equals the credit sum.

**Other protections:**
- Once a transaction is posted, its entries and the transaction itself cannot be inserted, updated, deleted or un-posted.
- Amounts must be positive integers in cents, and `side` must be `'D'` or `'C'`.
- An entry's account currency must match the transaction's currency.
- Foreign keys are enforced. Each connection must run `PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON`.
- The balance rule applies when a transaction is posted, not when its entries are inserted. A client that never posts leaves a draft, which the balance queries ignore.

**Queries in `queries.sql`:**
- Posting pattern.
- Account balances with natural sign.
- Trial balance.
- Audit for any posted transaction that doesn't balance.
- Account statement with running balance.
- Draft imbalances.

I ran the first three queries inside `test_queries_run`. The statement, posting-pattern and draft-imbalance queries are untested.
