# Category theory in practice: constructions

Read when computing limits, colimits, adjunctions or monads in standard categories (moved from `category-theory` SKILL.md).

## 2. Limits and colimits in standard categories
| Category | product | coproduct | equalizer of f,g: A ⇉ B | coequalizer | terminal / initial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set | A×B | disjoint union | {a : f a = g a} | B/∼ (equivalence relation generated by f a ∼ g a) | 1 / ∅ |
| Grp | direct product | free product A∗B | subgroup {f = g} | B/N, N the normal closure of {f(a)g(a)⁻¹} | trivial group (zero object) |
| Ab, R-Mod | direct product | direct sum (finite: biproduct) | ker(f − g) | B/im(f − g) | 0 (zero object) |
| CRing | product | A ⊗_ℤ B | subring {f = g} | B/(f(a) − g(a)) | zero ring / ℤ |
| Top | product topology | disjoint union | subspace | quotient topology | point / ∅ |
| Vect_k | direct product | direct sum | kernel of f − g | cokernel | 0 |
| poset P | meet (inf) | join (sup) | trivial (parallel arrows are equal) | trivial | top / bottom |
| [Cᵒᵖ, Set] | pointwise | pointwise | pointwise | pointwise | constant 1 / constant ∅ |

- Pullbacks: {(a,b) : f a = g b} in Set and Grp; the fiber product A ×_C B in CRing; its subspace
  topology in Top.
- In Top, U: Top → Set has a left adjoint (discrete) and a right adjoint (indiscrete), so it preserves
  limits and colimits: take the (co)limit of sets and give it the initial (final) topology.
- Existence and preservation:
  - Complete iff all small products and equalizers exist. Finite limits come from pullbacks plus a
    terminal object.
  - Limits commute with limits. Filtered colimits commute with finite limits in Set.
  - Representables C(A, −) preserve limits.
  - In functor categories, limits and colimits are computed pointwise wherever the target has them.
- Presheaves: every presheaf is a colimit of representables over its category of elements
  (density / co-Yoneda). For small C, [Cᵒᵖ, Set] is a topos.

## 3. Adjunctions
- F ⊣ G (F: C → D) can be given in three equivalent ways:
  1. D(FX, Y) ≅ C(X, GY), naturally in X and Y;
  2. a unit η: 1 ⇒ GF and counit ε: FG ⇒ 1 satisfying the triangle identities
     (εF)∘(Fη) = 1_F and (Gε)∘(ηG) = 1_G;
  3. for each X, a universal arrow X → GFX, initial in X ↓ G.
- Transposes: f: FX → Y ↦ G(f)∘η_X, and g: X → GY ↦ ε_Y∘F(g).
- Catalog to match against:
  - free ⊣ forgetful (groups, monoids, R-modules, rings, vector spaces);
  - discrete ⊣ U ⊣ indiscrete for Top → Set;
  - − × A ⊣ (−)^A (cartesian closed); − ⊗_R M ⊣ Hom_R(M, −);
  - colim ⊣ Δ ⊣ lim;
  - abelianization ⊣ inclusion Ab ↪ Grp; Stone–Čech β ⊣ inclusion CHaus ↪ Top;
    sheafification ⊣ inclusion;
  - extension ⊣ restriction ⊣ coextension of scalars (f_! ⊣ f^* ⊣ f_*);
  - ∃_f ⊣ f⁻¹ ⊣ ∀_f on powersets, and Σ_f ⊣ f^* ⊣ Π_f on slices;
  - Lan_K ⊣ K^* ⊣ Ran_K;
  - Galois connections between posets.
- Recognition:
  - "Freely add structure" suggests a left adjoint; "underlying" or "best approximation from inside"
    suggests a right adjoint.
  - A functor that fails to preserve some colimit is not a left adjoint. U: Grp → Set does not preserve
    coproducts, so it is not a left adjoint; it is a right adjoint.
- RAPL/LAPC: right adjoints preserve limits, left adjoints preserve colimits. Proof by Yoneda:
  C(X, G lim D) ≅ D(FX, lim D) ≅ lim D(FX, D−) ≅ lim C(X, GD−) ≅ C(X, lim GD).
- Existence (adjoint functor theorems), for G: D → C:
  - General (Freyd): D complete and locally small, G preserves small limits and satisfies the solution
    set condition. Then G has a left adjoint.
  - Special: C and D locally small, D complete, well-powered, with a cogenerating set, and G
    continuous. Then G has a left adjoint.
  - Locally presentable C and D: G is a right adjoint iff it preserves limits and is accessible
    (preserves κ-filtered colimits for some κ); F is a left adjoint iff it preserves colimits.
  - Mathlib: `isRightAdjoint_of_preservesLimits_of_solutionSetCondition`.
- Adjoints are unique up to unique isomorphism and compose. Squares of adjoints correspond via mates.
  Any equivalence upgrades to an adjoint equivalence.

## 5. Monads and comonads
- A monad (T, η, μ) satisfies μ∘Tμ = μ∘μT and μ∘Tη = 1 = μ∘ηT. Equivalently, it is a monoid in
  (End C, ∘, 1).
- Every adjunction F ⊣ G gives the monad (GF, η, GεF) on C and the comonad (FG, ε, FηG) on D.
- Kleisli category C_T: maps X → TY, with composition g ∘_K f = μ_Z∘T(g)∘f. It is the initial
  adjunction resolving T.
- Eilenberg–Moore category C^T: algebras (A, a: TA → A) with a∘η_A = 1 and a∘Ta = a∘μ_A. It is the
  terminal resolution; the comparison functor K: D → C^T links them.
- Beck's monadicity theorem: G is monadic iff it has a left adjoint, reflects isomorphisms, and D has,
  and G preserves, coequalizers of G-split pairs.
- Algebras of standard monads: list (free monoid) → monoids; covariant powerset → complete
  join-semilattices; ultrafilter monad on Set → compact Hausdorff spaces (Manes); finite distributions
  → convex spaces. The Giry monad does probability on measurable spaces.
- Programming monads: maybe/exception, state (−×S)^S, reader, writer. Comonads: stream, store,
  environment (−×E).
